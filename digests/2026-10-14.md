### [**AutoGPTQ 0.9 Released with Optimized Triton Kernels for Consumer VRAM**]

* **Summary**: The AutoGPTQ maintainers just dropped version 0.9, bringing rewritten Triton execution kernels that drastically cut memory overhead during 4-bit and 8-bit model weight loading. This update targets mid-range consumer GPUs—specifically optimizing inference throughput on 6GB and 8GB VRAM laptop setups commonly used by local developers. It bridges the gap between raw Hugging Face transformers and low-latency local execution without requiring enterprise server hardware. Check the release notes and benchmarks on the [AutoGPTQ GitHub Repository](https://github.com/AutoGPTQ/AutoGPTQ).
* **Practical Student Takeaway**: If your student laptop struggles with OOM errors while testing language models, switch your local RAG experiments to these new AutoGPTQ Triton kernels. This directly connects to your **Operating Systems** coursework on memory pagination and paging thrashing; understanding how tensor parallel operations map to limited physical VRAM will help you optimize batch sizes during local AI Lab testing.

### [**Telkomsel Innovation Center (TINC) Batch 9 Open-Source IoT & Edge AI Grant**]

* **Summary**: TINC has officially opened applications for its Batch 9 incubation track, focusing entirely on resource-constrained Edge AI and localized IoT distributed processing. Indonesian undergraduates can apply as teams to secure seed funding, cloud credits, and direct mentorship from Telkomsel’s engineering leads. Applications close on **15 November 2026**, and teams must submit a working GitHub prototype demonstrating low-latency local inference. Full guidelines and submission portals are live at the [TINC Indonesia Portal](https://tinc.id).
* **Practical Student Takeaway**: To make your CV stand out for this grant, push a clean, well-documented repository featuring a lightweight model deployed via Docker to a Raspberry Pi or local edge device. This project maps directly to your **Distributed Systems** curriculum regarding consensus algorithms and edge-node synchronization, proving to judges that you can build practical, bandwidth-efficient systems for Indonesia's infrastructure.
